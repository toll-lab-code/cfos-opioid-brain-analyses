Changes in v1.1.0 (relative to v1.0.0)
======================================
Prepared 2026-09-29 for the revised manuscript; finalized 2026-10-30 for the
Psychopharmacology submission (figure numbering, documentation).

No regional, network or cartographic statistical model was changed. The two
analysis scripts gained new sections; the figure script changed what the
whole-brain panels display and where they read their statistics from.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
regional_analysis.R
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
NEW SECTION "SUPPLEMENTARY 4: Whole-brain (global) activation".
  Computes three per-animal global measures -- the raw whole-brain count, the
  mean log10 regional density (the per-animal global offset), and the same
  restricted to regions labeled in every animal -- each with pooled and per-sex
  one-way ANOVA plus Tukey HSD on all six pairwise condition contrasts, and a
  two-way condition x sex ANOVA. Writes Drug_Global_Activation.xlsx.

  Rationale: these statistics were previously computed inside the figure script
  at run time, in two places and by two different tests, and appeared in no
  deposited workbook. They are now computed once and read by the figures.

  Comments describing the global analysis were brought in line with that section.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
regional_figures.R
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Figure 2 Panel A inclusion rule made symmetric. A region is plotted if it
  survives FDR (q < 0.05) in EITHER sex. The previous rule additionally admitted
  regions significant at uncorrected p < 0.05 in FEMALES ONLY, applying a laxer
  standard to one sex (8 regions admitted; 57 male regions meeting the identical
  standard excluded). The panel goes from 28 to 20 regions.

Whole-brain panels (Figure 2B, Figure S1C) now READ their statistics from
  Drug_Global_Activation.xlsx instead of recomputing them, so the panels cannot
  drift from the analysis. The in-script re-summing of per-animal totals was
  removed.

Those panels display global activation as the per-animal mean log10 regional
  density rather than the raw summed whole-brain count, following the convention
  in this literature (comparator studies report and analyse c-Fos density,
  log-transformed, and state global figures as mean densities).

Both panels now annotate Tukey HSD contrasts. Figure 2B previously used Welch
  t-tests with a Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment -- a different family from the
  Tukey p values quoted in the text. Figure S1C previously showed only the
  omnibus ANOVA and now also carries the pairwise brackets.

network_analysis.R
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
NEW SECTION "CO-ACTIVATION CONTROLS". Writes 04_Coactivation_Controls.xlsx.

  A. n-MATCHED SUBSAMPLING. Every condition subsampled to n_sub animals per sex,
     the network rebuilt from scratch per draw (the within-sex centering is
     recomputed inside each draw, so it never uses animals outside it), mean |r|
     recomputed over n_sub_draws draws; mean and 2.5th-97.5th percentiles.

  B. PER-ANIMAL GLOBAL OFFSET. Mean |r| with and without each animal's global
     offset removed, for the Combined network and for each sex; a label-
     permutation test on the mean |r| difference that shuffles condition labels
     WITHIN sex and rebuilds the whole network on each permutation; and the
     edge-wise Fisher r-to-z comparison on the offset-removed networks with
     counts split by direction.

  Both analyses were previously performed outside the deposited code, so the
  values they produce could not be regenerated by a reader.

run_fisher_z_test() gained an optional df_off argument (default unchanged).
  The offset-removed edge test uses n - 4, as the main analysis does: the
  within-sex row centering constrains each region across animals and so costs a
  degree of freedom from an across-animal correlation, whereas removing the
  per-animal offset centers each animal across regions and does not.

n_perm lowered from 5000 to 1000 to match the run that produced the reported
  modularity values.

regional_analysis.R gained a new section, SUPPLEMENTARY 5: Region-count
  permutation calibration, and a new output workbook,
  Drug_Region_Count_Permutation.xlsx.

  The sex-stratified analyses report counts of regions reaching Tukey p < 0.05
  that do not survive FDR correction across the 198 regions. Because the regions
  are strongly correlated, such a count cannot be read against the nominal 5%
  expectation. The new section permutes condition labels WITHIN sex, recomputes
  the per-region ANOVA and Tukey contrast on each permutation, and places each
  observed count in the resulting null distribution. It reports, for every
  stratum (pooled, male, female) and every pairwise contrast, the observed
  count, the null median, 95th and 99th percentiles, the null maximum, and an
  add-one permutation p-value.

  This calibration was previously performed outside the deposited code, so the
  p-values quoted in the manuscript could not be regenerated by a reader. It now
  runs at 10,000 permutations rather than the 1,000 used previously, because the
  female acute-morphine p-value sits near 0.05, where 1,000 permutations carry
  a Monte-Carlo standard error of about 0.007.

  For speed the null uses the closed-form Tukey-Kramer statistic rather than
  aov() + TukeyHSD(): a region's residual degrees of freedom are invariant under
  permutation, so the critical value can be computed once per region and reused.
  The closed form is checked against the aov()/TukeyHSD() counts of the primary
  and by-sex workbooks on every run, and the script stops if they disagree.

regional_analysis.R: the global-activation sensitivity analysis (SUPPLEMENTARY 3)
  now also runs WITHIN SEX, writing Drug_Statistical_Results_GlobalNorm_BySex.xlsx,
  and Drug_GlobalNorm_Concordance.xlsx gained two sheets: a by-sex comparison of
  density against globally normalized counts, and the cross-sex correlation of the
  per-region effects under both analyses.

  The Supplement previously reported that 6 regions reached significance in
  females under global normalization. They do not: no region survives FDR
  correction in either sex. What does hold is that the direction reverses at the
  uncorrected threshold (males 82 regions to 13, females 9 to 24), and the text
  now says that instead.

  The cross-sex correlations are reported WITHOUT a correction for measurement
  error. The Supplement previously gave disattenuated values of 0.82 and 0.89;
  at n = 5-6 per sex the reliability of a per-sex effect vector cannot be
  estimated stably (on the Cohen's d scale the estimate is at or below zero), and
  the correction roughly doubles the correlation. The observed values, which are
  lower bounds, are reported instead.

network_analysis.R gained a leave-one-animal-out hub jackknife, writing
  Hub_Jackknife_Summary and Hub_Jackknife_ByRegion sheets into
  03_Modules_and_Roles.xlsx. Reviewer 3 (major 5) asked for an assessment of how
  stable hub identity is across animals; this was previously computed outside the
  deposited code, so the Jaccard indices and recovery percentages quoted in the
  manuscript could not be regenerated.

  Each animal is dropped in turn, the Combined network is rebuilt from the two
  reduced per-sex matrices so the within-sex centering is recomputed on the animals
  that remain, re-thresholded to the same matched density, and the hub set
  recomputed. It reuses threshold_to_density() and add_hub_scores(), so the
  jackknife hub sets are identical in definition to the published ones; the script
  also recomputes the full-sample hub set the same way and warns if it does not
  match the published set. Jackknife replicates share n-1 animals and are not
  independent, so the output is reported as a stability diagnostic and not as a
  test of difference between conditions.

network_analysis.R gained a variance decomposition in the CO-ACTIVATION CONTROLS
  section, writing a Variance_decomposition sheet into
  04_Coactivation_Controls.xlsx: the share of variance carried by the per-animal
  global offset in each condition, with the offset and region-specific SDs. It is
  computed on the same within-sex-centred matrix the network is built from. This
  was previously computed outside the deposited code; the vehicle figure was
  reported as 27.2% and is 29.0%, with SDs 0.178 and 0.266 rather than 0.188 and
  0.294. The other three conditions reproduce exactly.

regional_analysis.R gained a new section, SUPPLEMENTARY 6: Cross-condition
  regional effect-profile similarity, and a new output workbook,
  Drug_Profile_Similarity.xlsx. For each vehicle-referenced contrast the vector
  of per-region Cohen's d over the 198 regions is correlated with the others
  (Pearson with 95% CI, and Spearman), and each profile is regressed on the
  acute-morphine profile to give its amplitude (slope) and SD ratio, with
  counts of regions below vehicle. All three contrasts share the vehicle group,
  so the correlations are comparable with each other but not unbiased in
  absolute terms; the script and workbook say so.

network_analysis.R gained a scale-invariance check, writing a
  Scale_invariance sheet into 04_Coactivation_Controls.xlsx. It adds a fixed,
  reproducible per-region offset to the count matrices, rebuilds every Combined
  correlation matrix, and confirms the result is unchanged (max difference
  ~2e-15), which shows numerically that the networks are the same whether built
  from counts or from volume-normalized densities. The script stops if the two
  disagree by more than 1e-10.
